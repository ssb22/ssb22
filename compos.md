## Arrangements

Most of the arrangements that I have made for private use have been of other people’s works that are still in copyright, and therefore I cannot put them here. Even my arrangements of traditional Chinese melodies cannot be placed here because my [source material was a modern performance](cucos.md) which may have included copyrighted embellishments. However, I believe it is acceptable to post this [simple arrangement of an unidentified Nu Shu song](https://ssb22.user.srcf.net/compos/nushu.mid) as sung by an elderly survivor who wanted it published (2003). Nu Shu was a literature invented by women who were not allowed to study in the male-only schools in old China. I assumed that the style would *not* be “normal Chinese” and that the singer’s ornamentation was intentional. The Tsinghua scholars I was assisting had different views and their documentary contains a re-working of it.

## Silliness

Virtually any random sequence of notes can be made vaguely tuneful by means of rhythm. To demonstrate this, here is [Pi to 100 decimal places](https://ssb22.user.srcf.net/compos/pi.mid) (the last digit has been rounded up). You may find this easier to remember than the raw digits, but it has little practical use. The same technique might help you memorise a more secret string of random digits for a security situation, but please don’t use the digits of Pi for that.

You may also be interested in my [”white noise” MIDI file](https://ssb22.user.srcf.net/compos/noise.html).
